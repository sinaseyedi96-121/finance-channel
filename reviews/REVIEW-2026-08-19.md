### Compliance / Risk — round 2 (rebuttal)

**Keep these three risk issues; push back on the precision-focused points.** Raw decimal precision and one-touch levels are primarily data/chart issues already caught elsewhere. The bigger editorial risk is how uncertain information is framed:

1. **Deals are being presented as locked-in demand or revenue.**  
   Quotes: “**$100B OpenAI Ohio data centre deal — locks in AI accelerator demand**” and “**AI chip deal with Marvell adds up to $120B revenue**.”  
   **Directive:** Separate announced investment, capacity plans, warrants, and “up to” figures from contracted or realized revenue. Use wording such as “could support future demand” or “may broaden Google’s supplier options,” and identify execution/customer-concentration risk immediately.

2. **Valuation metrics are treated as conclusions rather than inputs.**  
   Quotes: “**PEG 0.94… screams quality**,” “**PEG 0.44 + $100B AI target outweigh near-term tape**,” and “**AI capex… is likely a buying opportunity**.”  
   **Directive:** Replace certainty (“undervalued,” “outweighs,” “likely a buying opportunity”) with conditional language: “looks inexpensive if growth estimates hold,” “the bull case depends on…,” or “could offer support if….” A PEG or analyst target does not establish a floor or validate the thesis.

3. **The hidden-value post overstates speculative, binary stories.**  
   Quotes: “**The AI build-out’s real bottleneck… is the physical layer underneath**,” “**market is pricing in failure, not the option**,” “**IONQ… [could be] the consolidation winner**,” and “**rearmament budgets… are secular, not cyclical**.”  
   **Directive:** Present these as watchlist hypotheses, not discovered mispricing. For pre-revenue/development-stage names, lead with permitting, financing, dilution, runway, execution, and commodity-price uncertainty; explicitly say analyst upside is scenario-dependent rather than evidence of re-rating potential.
